# Charging and Battery Guide

> Fictional sample data: this guide does not describe any real manufacturer,
> vehicle, charging network, or customer policy.

## Everyday charging

For routine driving, set the charge target to 80 percent. Charging to 100 percent
is appropriate before a long journey, but the vehicle should be driven soon after
it reaches that level. Leaving the traction battery at 100 percent for several
days can accelerate normal battery ageing. When storing the vehicle for more than
two weeks, leave it between 50 and 60 percent, unplug non-essential accessories,
and check the charge level every 30 days.

The vehicle accepts alternating-current charging through its standard inlet and
direct-current fast charging through the compatible fast-charge connector. The
portable charging cable is intended for occasional use from a correctly earthed,
undamaged outlet. Do not use an extension lead, multi-board, travel adaptor, or
outlet that becomes hot. A licensed electrician should inspect any outlet used
regularly for vehicle charging.

## Before connecting

Before every charge, inspect the cable and connector for cracks, bent pins,
moisture, corrosion, or heat damage. Confirm that the connector is compatible
with the vehicle inlet and follow the safety instructions displayed by the
charger. Do not connect damaged equipment. If the inlet or connector is wet from
ordinary rain, wipe away standing water; never charge in floodwater or while the
vehicle is partly submerged.

Insert the connector fully until the vehicle confirms that it is locked. The
charge-status light pulses blue while energy is flowing and turns solid green
when the selected target is reached. A red light means charging has stopped.
Disconnect once, inspect both ends, and try a different known-good charger. If a
red light returns, record the time and displayed error, then arrange support.

## Fast charging and temperature

Repeated fast charging is allowed, although alternating-current charging is the
preferred routine method when time permits. Fast-charge power normally tapers
above 80 percent to protect the battery, so the final 20 percent may take as long
as the earlier portion. A charger advertising a high maximum output does not
guarantee that the vehicle will accept that rate.

In cold weather, use navigation to select the fast charger at least 20 minutes
before arrival. The vehicle will precondition the battery when sufficient energy
is available. In very hot weather, park in shade when practical and allow the
thermal system to run; fan or pump noise after parking is normal. Extreme
temperature, a high charge level, and charger sharing can all reduce charging
speed without indicating a fault.

## Low charge and loss of power

At 10 percent, the display recommends a nearby compatible charger. At 5 percent,
power and climate performance may be limited. Estimated range is guidance, not a
guarantee; speed, hills, load, wind, and temperature affect consumption. Drivers
should not plan to arrive with less than a 10 percent reserve.

If the traction battery becomes depleted, move away from traffic if the vehicle
can still be driven safely, switch on the hazard lights, and request roadside
assistance through the in-vehicle Help menu. Do not attempt to jump-start,
push-charge, or tow-charge the high-voltage battery. Roadside assistance may
transport the vehicle to the nearest compatible public charger or authorised
service location; the separate roadside guide defines eligibility and exclusions.

## Battery health

Some gradual loss of usable capacity is expected. Range shown on the display is
not itself a capacity test. The battery warranty uses a service diagnostic and a
70 percent retained-capacity threshold during its stated time and distance
limits. Charging history cannot by itself approve or reject a warranty claim.
Keep software current because energy-estimation and thermal-control improvements
can change displayed range without changing physical battery capacity.
