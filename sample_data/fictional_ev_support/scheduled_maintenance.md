# Scheduled Maintenance

> Fictional sample data: this schedule is for a made-up support corpus and must
> not be used to maintain any real vehicle.

## Standard schedule

Schedule a maintenance inspection every 12 months or 20,000 kilometres,
whichever occurs first. The inspection includes tyres, steering, suspension,
exterior lights, charging inlet, high-voltage cable covers, low-voltage battery,
brake operation, and visible coolant leaks. Electric vehicles do not require
engine-oil or spark-plug changes.

Rotate the tyres every 10,000 kilometres when tread condition permits. Rotation
may be brought forward when front-to-rear wear differs by more than 1.5
millimetres. Tyre pressure should be checked monthly and before a long trip using
the value on the vehicle placard, not the maximum printed on the tyre.

Replace the cabin air filter every 24 months or 40,000 kilometres. Inspect brake
fluid every 24 months and replace it when the moisture test reaches the service
limit. Inspect battery thermal coolant at each annual visit; its first scheduled
replacement is at five years, then every three years. A leak, warning, or
contamination requires earlier service.

## Severe-use checks

Vehicles routinely operated on unsealed roads, in deep road salt, in standing
water below the marked limit, or under repeated maximum payload should receive an
intermediate inspection at six months or 10,000 kilometres. The intermediate
visit checks underbody shields, brake movement, suspension joints, and charging
inlet seals. It does not replace the 12-month inspection.

After driving through road salt or coastal spray, rinse the body and accessible
underbody with low-pressure fresh water. Do not direct a pressure washer into the
charging inlet, seals, cameras, or high-voltage components. Collision, flood, or
underbody impact requires an inspection even if no warning is shown.

## Brake and tyre care

Regenerative braking reduces friction-brake use, which can allow surface
corrosion to build up. When conditions are safe, follow the in-vehicle brake-care
prompt to perform several moderate friction-brake applications. Grinding,
pulling, vibration, fluid loss, or a persistent brake warning requires service
rather than a cleaning procedure.

Only tyres with the correct size, load rating, speed rating, and electric-vehicle
load suitability should be fitted. Uneven wear can reduce range and may indicate
alignment or suspension damage. Tyres and normal alignment are wear items, but a
separate confirmed component defect may fall under warranty.

## Records and alerts

Keep invoices showing the date, odometer, work performed, parts, and fluid
specifications. Service records may be uploaded to the support portal, including
work performed by a qualified independent repairer. Records help diagnose faults
and demonstrate that missed maintenance did not cause a later issue.

The vehicle can estimate when an inspection is due, but its reminder does not
replace the time-and-distance limits. Reset the reminder only after the work is
completed. Software updates may improve reminders or diagnostics; they do not
erase overdue maintenance.

## Relationship to other support

Maintenance is not automatically included with the vehicle purchase, roadside
assistance, or limited warranty. If a fault makes the vehicle unsafe to drive,
stop safely and request roadside assistance rather than continuing to a scheduled
appointment. A maintenance visit may identify a warrantable defect, but the
service team records the maintenance work and warranty claim separately.
